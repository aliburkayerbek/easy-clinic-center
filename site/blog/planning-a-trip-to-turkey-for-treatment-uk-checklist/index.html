Planning a Trip to Turkey for Treatment: A UK Patient Checklist

2026-10-18 · 5 min read

Travelling abroad for treatment involves more than booking a flight. You will need the right documents, a plan for recovery, and a clear understanding of what is included in your quote. This checklist covers the practical side of a trip to Turkey from the UK. Rules change, so always confirm the latest requirements from official sources.

Key takeaways

  • Check your passport and visa requirements well before you book.
  • Turkey is on GMT+3 all year, which matters for calls and medication timings.
  • Bring a companion if you can, and pack for a recovery, not a holiday.
  • Understand what your quote includes: Option A (all-inclusive) or Option B (treatment only).
  • Keep a full set of records to bring home.

Passport and visa

Passport. Check current requirements before you travel. At the time of writing, our project note is that a passport should be valid for at least 150 days from your date of arrival, but entry rules can change, so confirm this with official guidance rather than relying on this article. Do this early, because renewing a passport takes time.

Visa. British citizens can generally visit for up to 90 days in any 180 without a visa for tourism or business. Medical travel may be treated differently, so check with the Turkish embassy or consulate before you go. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office also publishes travel advice for the country.

Time zone and contact

Türkiye uses GMT+3 throughout the year, with no clock change. Remember that it is two hours ahead of UK time in winter and one hour ahead in summer, which affects when you can call your GP, family or insurer. Ask your coordinator which hours the team is available and how to reach someone out of hours.

Who to bring

Where possible, bring a relative or friend. A companion can:

  • Help you after the procedure, when you may be tired or limited in movement.
  • Listen to instructions and remember them, which is hard after anaesthesia.
  • Handle luggage, transport and paperwork.

If you travel alone, tell someone at home your schedule and keep in regular contact. Ask the provider what support is available.

When to travel

Do not plan back-to-back commitments around your trip. Ask the surgeon how long you need to stay before it is safe to fly home, and add some buffer in case recovery takes longer. Avoid booking a non-refundable return flight until the stay is confirmed. Our article on questions to ask before treatment abroad lists what to ask about recovery and flying.

What to pack

Pack for comfort and practicality.

  • Documents: passport, any visa paperwork, insurance details, and your quote and confirmation emails.
  • Medical records: printed and digital copies of letters, reports and imaging. See our guide to preparing medical records.
  • Medication: enough for the whole stay, in original packaging, with a list of names and doses.
  • Clothing: loose, front-opening, easy-to-wear clothes, and comfortable shoes, depending on the procedure.
  • Extras: phone charger, adaptor, toiletries, and anything your surgeon has specifically recommended.

Check airline rules on carrying medication, and keep essential items in your hand luggage.

Quote options: A and B

After you apply, you receive a written quote from the hospital. It comes in two forms.

  • Option A, all-inclusive. The quote covers treatment, flights, hotel and transfers, so most of the arrangements are handled in one package.
  • Option B, treatment only. The quote covers the treatment itself. Travel, accommodation and transfers are optional extras, or you arrange them yourself.

Neither is better in every case. Option A suits people who want fewer decisions to make. Option B suits those who prefer to choose their own flights and accommodation. Whichever you choose, ask for an itemised breakdown so that you know exactly what you are paying for.

Money, phone and local help

Tell your bank you will be travelling so that card payments are not blocked. Check roaming charges with your mobile provider, or consider a local SIM or an eSIM. Save the contact details of your coordinator, the hospital and your insurer in your phone, and keep a paper copy too. Keep a note of the hospital address in Latin letters so that a taxi driver can find it easily.

On arrival and during recovery

Allow yourself a quiet first day to rest after the journey. Keep all appointments for dressing changes and check-ups, and do not travel home until your surgeon has confirmed that it is safe. If anything worries you, such as fever, increasing pain, swelling or bleeding, contact the team straight away rather than waiting. Before you leave, collect your discharge summary, operation notes, medication list and instructions for aftercare, so your GP has what they need when you return.

Insurance and your GP

Check what your travel insurance covers, and whether it includes complications from planned treatment. Tell your GP about your plans before you go. Our guide to plastic surgery in Turkey discusses recovery and risks in more detail for those considering that route.
