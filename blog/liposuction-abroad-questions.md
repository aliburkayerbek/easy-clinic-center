Liposuction is a surgical procedure that removes fat from specific areas of the body. Some UK patients consider having it abroad, often because of cost or availability. This article is a checklist of questions to help you decide carefully. It does not describe results, promise any outcome, or present liposuction as a way to lose weight. What is suitable for you depends on your health, your body and the advice of a qualified surgeon who has examined you.

## Key takeaways

- Liposuction is an operation, with the risks that come with anaesthetic and surgery. It should follow a proper medical assessment.
- Be wary of anyone who promises particular results or confirms you are suitable from photographs alone.
- Ask about the surgeon, the facility and who is responsible for your care afterwards.
- Get the plan and quote in writing, including what is and is not included.
- Ask the surgeon about aftercare and compression garments, and let the surgical team decide when you can travel.

## Start with the assessment

A responsible team will want to know about your health before offering a date. Expect questions about your medical history, medicines, previous operations and any conditions that might affect anaesthetic or healing. You should be able to ask:

- Who will assess me, and will I speak to the surgeon who will operate?
- What information do you need from me, such as records or test results?
- Is liposuction the right option for what I want, and what other options exist?
- What would make you decide that I should not have this operation, or should wait?
- What tests are done before surgery, and who explains the results to me?

Liposuction is generally described as a body-contouring procedure, not a treatment for weight loss or a substitute for other health care. Ask the surgeon to explain what it can and cannot do in your case, in plain language. Treat any offer that sounds too definite, or that relies on before-and-after images, with caution. Appearance after surgery varies between people, and no one can guarantee it.

## Questions about the surgeon and facility

You are entitled to know who will operate on you and where. Ask:

- What are the surgeon's qualifications and specialty, and where can I verify them?
- How often does the surgeon perform this operation, and will the same surgeon operate on me?
- Which hospital or facility will be used, and is it a fully equipped hospital with the staff and equipment needed to deal with problems?
- Who gives the anaesthetic, and will I meet them beforehand?
- What happens if there is an emergency during or after the operation?
- Who will care for me overnight or during my stay?

Ask for these details in writing, and take time to check them. ## Get the plan and quote in writing

Before paying a deposit, ask for a written plan. It should name the proposed procedure, the areas involved, the surgeon, the hospital and the type of anaesthetic. It should also say what happens if the team decides not to go ahead. Then look closely at the quote. Ask:

- What does the price include, for example consultation, tests, the operation, hospital stay, medicines, garments, accommodation and transfers?
- What is excluded or charged separately?
- What happens, and who pays, if extra care is needed?
- What is the deposit, is it refundable, and under what conditions?
- Is there a written policy for complications or revision procedures, and what are its limits?

Our guide to [how to read a hospital quote](/blog/how-to-read-a-hospital-quote/) shows how to compare quotes fairly. If anything is unclear, ask for it to be explained in writing rather than accepting a verbal answer.

## Aftercare and compression garments

Recovery is part of the operation, and the arrangements should be clear before you travel. After liposuction, surgeons often give specific instructions about dressings, garments, activity and review appointments. Those instructions differ between surgeons and patients, so do not rely on general websites. Put these questions to your surgeon:

- Will I need a compression garment, which type, and for how long? Is it included in the quote?
- What care will my wounds need, and who will check them?
- When will I be reviewed, and by whom?
- What activity should I avoid, and for how long?
- Which symptoms should make me contact the team straight away?
- How can I reach the team after I leave, and how quickly will they reply?

Ask for the answers in writing. Our article on [aftercare when having surgery abroad](/blog/aftercare-when-having-surgery-abroad/) explains how to plan follow-up with your GP and your overseas team. Speak to your GP before you travel, and make sure your operation note and discharge summary will be sent to them.

## Let the surgical team decide on travel

Do not book fixed flights, hotels or time off work until the surgical team has told you what they need. When you can safely fly home after surgery is a medical decision for your case. Choose flexible tickets where possible, avoid tight schedules, and ask for the fly-home advice in writing. Check your travel insurance too, because many policies exclude planned treatment and related complications. Our guide to [medical travel insurance in the UK](/blog/medical-travel-insurance-uk/) explains what to look for.

If you feel unwell after surgery, contact your treating team or your GP. For urgent advice in the UK use NHS 111, and in an emergency call 999 or go to A&E.
