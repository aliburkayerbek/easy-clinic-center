A quote for treatment abroad can look reassuring, with one clear number and a friendly message. But a single figure tells you very little unless you know what stands behind it. Two quotes for the same procedure may cover quite different things. This guide explains what a clear written quote should state, how to tell an all-inclusive offer from a treatment-only one, and which warning signs to watch for. It does not say what any treatment should cost, and it does not recommend any provider.

## Key takeaways

- A quote should be in writing and should name the procedure, the hospital and the treating surgeon or doctor.
- Check carefully what is included and what is excluded, and ask about extra costs that might arise.
- Look at the validity date, payment terms, and cancellation and refund conditions before paying anything.
- Understand the difference between an all-inclusive package and a treatment-only quote.
- Vague wording, pressure to pay quickly and missing follow-up details are warning signs.

## What a clear written quote should state

Think of a quote as the starting point for your decision, not a sales message. A clear one usually contains:

1. **The procedure.** The exact name of the treatment or operation, and ideally a short description of what it involves.
2. **The hospital or clinic.** Its name and location, so you know where treatment will happen.
3. **The surgeon or treating doctor.** Who will carry out the procedure, and what their specialty is. If it is not yet decided, the quote should say so.
4. **What is included.** For example tests, theatre and anaesthesia, hospital stay, medicines, dressings, supports or garments, and follow-up appointments.
5. **What is excluded.** Anything not covered, such as additional investigations or travel.
6. **Possible extra costs.** What might change the total, and how you would be told before it did.
7. **Validity.** The date until which the quote stands.
8. **Payment terms.** The amount and timing of any deposit and balance, accepted methods, and the currency.
9. **Cancellation and refund terms.** What happens if you cancel, if the clinical team decides not to operate, or if the plan changes.
10. **Follow-up.** What aftercare is included, for how long, and how you contact the team afterwards.

If any of these items is missing, ask for it before you proceed. A provider who is confident in their arrangements should be happy to put them on paper.

## Questions about inclusions and extras

Read the inclusions list slowly, then ask what is not on it. Some useful questions are:

- Are pre-operative tests included, and what if more are needed?
- Does the quote cover the hospital stay, and for how many nights?
- What if I need to stay longer because of medical advice?
- Are medicines, dressings and supports included, or only some?
- Does the quote cover a review before I travel home?
- Are interpreter, transfers and airport pick-up included?
- What happens if the team decides on a different or additional procedure once they have examined me?

For the last question, the quote should explain how any change would be agreed in writing, and who decides. Our article on [questions to ask before treatment abroad](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-treatment-abroad/) contains a wider checklist.

## Validity, payment and cancellation

Do not leave these until after you have paid. Check:

- **Validity.** How long the quote stands, and whether it can be extended.
- **Deposit.** How much is asked for, what it secures, and whether it is refundable.
- **Balance.** When it is due, and in which currency. Currency movements can matter if the quote is not in pounds.
- **Cancellation by you.** What is refunded and what is kept at different stages.
- **Cancellation by the clinical team.** What happens if the surgeon decides the treatment is not suitable once they have seen your records or examined you.
- **Changes.** How dates can be moved and what it costs, if anything.

Ask for the terms in writing, and keep copies of every document and message. Where you are paying by card, consider checking with your card provider about protection, and ask your insurer about trip and treatment cover. ## Red flags

None of these proves that a provider is unsafe, but each is a reason to slow down and ask more questions.

- A price offered without any request for your medical history or records.
- No named hospital or doctor, or an unwillingness to say who will treat you.
- Pressure to pay a deposit quickly because of a limited-time offer.
- Wording such as "everything included" with no list of what that means.
- No information on follow-up, or on what happens if there are complications.
- Refund terms that are missing, unclear or only given verbally.
- Promises of guaranteed results.
