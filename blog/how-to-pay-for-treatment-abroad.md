Paying for treatment abroad can feel different from paying for care at home. You may be sending money to an unfamiliar organisation, in a different currency, before you have met anyone in person. Good habits protect you. This guide sets out practical steps UK patients can take to pay for medical treatment abroad more safely. It does not recommend any provider or payment method, and it does not tell you what treatment should cost.

## Key takeaways

- Get a written quote and written payment terms before you pay anything.
- Ask exactly what is refundable, when, and in what circumstances.
- Keep receipts, confirmations and bank records for everything you pay.
- Be wary of pressure to pay quickly, or to pay in cash.
- Ask your bank or card provider what protections apply, rather than assuming, and ask your insurer about travel and cancellation cover.

## Start with a written quote and terms

Never pay on the strength of a conversation or a message alone. Ask for a written quote that names the procedure, the hospital, the treating doctor, what is included and what is not. Then ask for the payment terms in writing, covering:

- how much is due, and when
- what any deposit secures
- which payment methods are accepted, and in which currency
- who the payment goes to, with the full name of the organisation
- the validity date of the quote

Read the quote at home, away from any sales conversation. Our guide on [how to read a hospital quote](/blog/how-to-read-a-hospital-quote/) explains what each section should say. If a provider cannot or will not put terms in writing, that is a reason to stop and think.

At Easy Clinic Center, a health consultancy in Istanbul, Türkiye, we give written quotes as Option A (all-inclusive: treatment, flights, accommodation and transfers) or Option B (treatment only). Whoever you deal with, make sure you know which of these you are being offered.

## Ask what is refundable and when

Refund terms are easy to overlook until something changes. Before you pay, ask:

- What is refundable if I cancel, and at what point do the conditions change?
- What happens if the medical team decides after reviewing my records that treatment is not suitable?
- What happens if my dates need to move?
- What happens if I become unwell before travel?
- How long would a refund take, and how would it be paid?

Get the answers in writing and keep them with the quote. Terms stated only verbally can be hard to rely on later. If there is a deposit, ask whether it is separate from the balance and what it covers.

## Keep a paper trail

Records protect you if there is ever a disagreement. Keep:

- the signed or accepted quote and any later versions
- all emails and messages about payment and changes
- receipts and payment confirmations
- bank or card statements showing each payment
- any invoices, with dates and amounts

Store digital copies somewhere safe and keep a set to bring with you. If anything is paid in a different currency, note the exchange rate and any charges your bank applied. Do not delete messages after treatment; you may need them if questions arise later.

## Be wary of pressure and cash

Some warning signs relate to how you are asked to pay rather than how much. Slow down if you notice:

- pressure to pay a deposit quickly because an offer is about to end
- requests to pay in cash, or to send money to a personal account rather than an organisation's
- payment instructions that change at the last minute, especially by message
- a reluctance to provide a receipt or invoice
- refusal to explain how or why a payment is needed

None of these proves that something is wrong, but each is a reason to ask more questions. If payment details are changed, check them by contacting the organisation on a number or address you already trust, not on details in the new message. Never send money before you have read and understood the terms.

## Ask your bank or card provider

Different payment methods may carry different protections, and the rules that apply depend on the method, the provider and the circumstances. We cannot tell you what covers you, and you should not assume that you are protected. Instead, contact your bank or card provider before you pay and ask:

- Whether and how a payment of this kind is protected, and what the conditions are
- Whether there are limits on international payments or extra fees
- What happens if the service is not provided, or is provided differently from what was agreed
- How to dispute a payment, and what evidence they would want

Ask for their answer in writing if you can. Also check for any fees on foreign payments, and whether paying in sterling or in another currency makes a difference to you.

## Insurance and unexpected costs

Think about what could change after you pay. Travel and cancellation insurance may cover some situations, but policies differ and many exclude planned medical treatment unless specifically agreed. Ask your insurer directly what is covered, what is excluded and whether you need to tell them about the planned treatment. Our article on [travel insurance for treatment abroad](/blog/medical-travel-insurance-uk/) lists useful questions.

Also ask the hospital how unexpected costs are handled. For example, what happens if you need extra tests, a longer stay or an additional procedure? The answer should explain how any change is agreed, who decides and how you would be told before any extra charge.
