Many people from the UK now look at plastic and aesthetic surgery in Türkiye. The reasons vary, and so does the quality of the information available online. This guide does not recommend any clinic or promise any result. It is meant to help you decide whether to go ahead, and what to check if you do.

## Key takeaways

- Treat surgery as a medical decision, not a holiday purchase.
- Insist on a proper consultation and a written plan before you travel.
- Understand the recovery time and when you can safely fly home.
- Every operation has risks, and results differ from person to person.
- A second opinion beforehand can help you decide with more confidence.

## Start with the reason, not the destination

Before comparing countries or clinics, be clear about why you want the procedure and what you hope will change. Plastic surgery covers reconstructive work, such as repairing damage after injury or illness, and aesthetic work, which aims to change appearance. The medical questions are different in each case, so your first conversation should be about your goals and your health.

Think about whether you would still want the operation if it cost more or took longer. If the answer is no, give yourself more time.

## The consultation

A responsible provider will take the consultation seriously, even if it happens remotely.

- Expect questions about your medical history, medication, previous surgery, smoking and allergies.
- Ask which surgeon will perform the procedure and request their name and specialty in writing.
- Expect an honest discussion of what is and is not realistic for your body.
- Be wary of anyone who agrees to everything you ask for, or who pressures you to book quickly.

If the plan cannot be assessed properly without an examination, a good surgeon will say so. Our general checklist on [questions to ask before treatment abroad](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-treatment-abroad/) covers qualifications, accreditation and quotes in more detail.

## Realistic expectations

Surgery changes the body, but it cannot promise a particular outcome. Swelling, bruising and scars are expected, and final results can take months to appear. Be cautious about marketing that suggests guaranteed results or shows only the most successful cases. Photographs from other patients reflect their anatomy and healing, not yours.

Ask your surgeon:

- What is a realistic outcome for me?
- What are the limits of this procedure?
- Might I need a further operation later?

## Recovery and flying home

Recovery is the part people most often underestimate. Many procedures require you to stay near your surgeon for a period before it is safe to fly, and the length depends on the operation, your health and how you heal.

- Ask the surgeon for the minimum stay in writing, and plan for the possibility of staying longer.
- Ask when the first post-operative check will take place and who will do it.
- Ask about restrictions on lifting, driving, exercise and returning to work.
- Consider travelling with a companion who can help during the first days.

Longer flights soon after surgery can raise the risk of blood clots for some patients, so follow your surgeon's advice on timing.

## Risks to understand

All surgery carries risk. Depending on the procedure, these may include infection, bleeding, poor wound healing, scarring, reactions to anaesthesia, blood clots, or results that you are not happy with. Your own health, such as weight, smoking or other conditions, can raise some of these risks.

Ask what the plan is if a complication occurs, both during your stay and after you return to the UK. Ask who covers the cost of corrective treatment. Make sure you know how to contact the team and in what language.

## Check the practical details

- **Itemised quote.** Request a written breakdown of what is included.
- **Accreditation.** If a hospital claims accreditation, verify it on the accrediting body's website.
- **Insurance.** Check whether your travel policy covers complications from planned surgery.
- **Records.** Bring home the operation notes, implant details if relevant, and discharge summary.
- **Your GP.** Tell them about your plans before you go.

## Why a second opinion first can help

A second opinion lets an independent specialist look at your health and your proposed plan before you commit. It might confirm that the plan is reasonable, suggest questions to put to the surgeon, or highlight a health issue to address first. It does not replace a face-to-face assessment by your operating surgeon, but it can make your decision better informed.
